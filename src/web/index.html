Filename specifier reference

Specifiers are case-sensitive: %m is month; %M is minute. Examples use 2026-10-04 15:08:09.037 at UTC-04:00.

SpecifierMeaning and example
%YFour-digit year: 2026
%mMonth, zero-padded (01–12): 10
%dDay of month, zero-padded (01–31): 04
%HHour, 24-hour clock (00–23): 15
%MMinute, zero-padded (00–59): 08
%SSecond, zero-padded (00–59): 09
%FFull date, same as %Y-%m-%d: 2026-10-04
%1NTenths of a second (1 digit): 0
%2NHundredths of a second (2 digits): 03
%3NMilliseconds (3 digits): 037
%zLocal UTC offset, signed hours/minutes: -0400 (four hours behind UTC)
%sWhole seconds since 1970-01-01 00:00:00 UTC, rounded down: 1791140889
%%Literal percent sign: %

Fractions truncate without rounding. Write the decimal point yourself: %S.%3N produces 09.037.

merge--%F_%H%M%S.%3N%z.log produces merge--2026-10-04_150809.037-0400.log at the example time. report-100%%.log saves as report-100%.log.

Use %H%M%S and %z instead of the unsupported %T and %:z to avoid colons. Fractions above three digits and %N are unsupported because the clock has millisecond precision.

Automatic formats: %Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%S, %Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S, %Y/%m/%d %H:%M:%S. Optional dot/comma fractions (up to nine digits) and attached Z, ±HH:MM, or ±HHMM offsets.

Automatic detection consumes complete fractions and offsets and prefers one full timestamp over date-only message text, such as a BIOS date or --since 2026-09-08. Override specifiers: %Y %m %d %H %M %S, %1N through %9N (%N means nine digits), %:z (Z or colon offset), %z (compact offset), %F (date), %T (clock), and %% (literal %). Include a literal dot or comma before fractional digits. Override fields are required with exact widths. Nonblank overrides preserve literal leading and trailing spaces; a trailing literal separator may precede message text. Letters, punctuation, and brackets are literal, so [%F %T] matches a bracketed timestamp. Numeric day/month record timestamps need an override, for example %d/%m/%Y %H:%M:%S.

Filename templates use the same date-specifier syntax as format overrides. Filename specifiers are case-sensitive: %Y is the four-digit year, %m the month (01–12), %d the day of month (01–31), %H the 24-hour clock hour (00–23), %M the minute (00–59), and %S the second (00–59). Numeric fields are zero-padded. %F is the full date (%Y-%m-%d), such as 2026-10-04. %1N, %2N, and %3N are tenths, hundredths, and milliseconds: a fraction of .037 produces 0, 03, and 037. Write the decimal point yourself, such as %S.%3N. %z is the local UTC offset as signed hours/minutes, such as -0400 for four hours behind UTC. %s is whole seconds since 1970-01-01 00:00:00 UTC, rounded down. %% produces a literal percent sign: report-100%%.log saves as report-100%.log. Fractions truncate milliseconds; %s is Unix epoch seconds. Use %z instead of %:z and %H%M%S instead of %T to avoid colons; filename fractions above three digits and %N are unsupported.

Explicit epochs: epoch-s (optional decimal fraction), epoch-ms, epoch-us, epoch-ns (integers). Multiple matching timestamps on one line are errors. Epoch overrides reject recognizable calendar dates and leading clock-only records; joined values such as 123-456 are not standalone epochs.

One format per file. Complete dates only; named timezones and leap seconds are unsupported. Leading lines stay before their first entry; subsequent continuation lines follow their preceding entry. Nonempty files without timestamps are errors. Empty files are ignored.

Explicit timestamp offsets and epochs take precedence over File timezone, then the global Fallback timezone. File timezone accepts UTC or ±HH:MM, including +05:30 and +05:45; blank inherits the global fallback. If every nonempty selected file needs a timezone and none has an assignment, UTC is assumed with a warning. Mixed selections and partial assignments require a fallback for each unresolved file. Empty and excluded files do not affect this policy. Warnings are never included in downloads.

Time adjustment shifts every entry in that file after timezone interpretation. Positive means later; negative means earlier. Enter signed hours and minutes (+00:30, -01:00), or choose 30-minute/1-hour steps and use Earlier/Later. Hours can exceed 23; Reset sets +00:00. Remerge applies the edits without accumulating shifts. Original timestamp text remains unchanged in preview and download, so displayed timestamps may appear out of order. Downloads do not retain adjustment metadata. Fixed offsets and constant adjustments do not handle changing clock errors or daylight-saving transitions.
